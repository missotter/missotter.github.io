The air has turned crisp, carrying that unmistakable scent of woodsmoke and damp earth. Inside the studio, the light shifts earlier now, casting long golden angles across my desk. 

There is something deeply comforting about shifting seasonal rhythms. Summer's frantic energy—sketches made on crowded trains and sun-bleached notebooks—gives way to quiet indoor concentration. 

### Organizing the Palette

Today I spent hours washing porcelain palettes, sorting through dried pans of indigo and burnt sienna, and sharpening graphite pencils down to careful stubs. There is ritual in preparation. When your tools are clean and waiting, the barrier between thought and paper disappears.

* **Current project:** A series of small watercolor studies documenting autumn leaves found along the canal.
* **Reading:** Letters to a Young Poet by Rainer Maria Rilke.
* **Tea of the day:** Lapsang Souchong with a splash of oat milk.

As the days shorten, the work deepens. I look forward to sharing more of these quiet notes, sketches, and dispatches from the road as we move toward winter.
